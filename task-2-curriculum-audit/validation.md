## 2. Proposal presented

The proposal recommends targeted improvements to the B.Tech CSE (AI & ML) curriculum while retaining the 166-credit baseline:

- Increase Deep Learning (DS306) from 3 to 4 credits to support practical work.
- Increase Computer Vision (CS305) from 3 to 4 credits to support practical work.
- Reduce Digital Fabrication (MT101) from 3 to 2 credits and Introduction to Entrepreneurship (MG401) from 3 to 2 credits, subject to academic approval.
- Replace an existing 3-credit discipline-elective slot with a proposed Document Intelligence and Information Retrieval elective.
- Use the existing 3-credit Special Project (SP301) in Semester VI for the sports-ground booking platform, without introducing a new course or additional credits.
- Improve practical assessments, testing, evaluation, and project-based learning in selected existing subjects.

These are proposed changes, not approved university curriculum changes.

## 5. Strongest claim and what could disprove it

**Strongest claim:** Practical, assessment-level improvements across existing courses, combined with carefully selected credit reallocations, could better prepare students for entry-level AI and software engineering work without increasing the programme's total credits.

**Evidence that could weaken or disprove this claim:**

1. A comparison of the existing syllabus, assessment rubrics, and student work could show that the proposed practical outcomes are already taught and assessed adequately.
2. Employer or hiring-manager feedback could indicate that the proposed topics are not significant gaps for the selected roles.
3. A comparison of student performance before and after practical-assessment changes could show no meaningful improvement in relevant competencies.
4. Faculty workload, accreditation requirements, prerequisite dependencies, or programme regulations could demonstrate that the credit changes are impractical or would weaken essential learning outcomes.
5. A review of the subjects proposed for credit reductions could show that their existing learning outcomes are necessary and cannot reasonably be achieved in fewer credits.

**How to test the claim:** Obtain faculty review, compare the current and proposed learning outcomes, and pilot the revised practical assessments. Measure performance using common rubrics for implementation correctness, testing, debugging, evaluation, documentation, and explanation of technical decisions. Where possible, compare results with a previous cohort or an unchanged assessment baseline.
